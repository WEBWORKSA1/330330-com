Qixi: the original long-distance love story

Qixi (七夕) falls on the 7th day of the 7th lunar month. In the legend, Zhinü the weaver girl, a daughter of the heavens, falls in love with Niulang, a mortal cowherd. When they are discovered, the Queen Mother of the West draws the Milky Way across the sky to separate them. Once a year, on the seventh night of the seventh month, all the magpies of the world fly up to form a bridge so the two can meet.

Look up on a clear Qixi night and you can see them: Vega (Zhinü) and Altair (Niulang), on opposite banks of the Milky Way. That's why Qixi is the unofficial patron festival of every long-distance couple — and why 330330's colours borrow from the night sky.

Mid-Autumn: same moon, different cities

The Song-dynasty poet Su Shi wrote 但愿人长久，千里共婵娟 — "May we live long, and though a thousand miles apart, share the beauty of the moon." It is still the most-sent line between families apart at Mid-Autumn. Send it with a mooncake box and a call at moonrise.
