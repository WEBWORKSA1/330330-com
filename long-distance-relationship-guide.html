The long-distance relationship guide

Distance doesn't doom a relationship. Lack of a plan does. Here's what the numbers say and what to actually do.

What the data says

~14MAmericans in LDRs
~3Mmarried US couples living apart
343texts per week, on average
75%of engaged couples were long-distance at some point

Surveys compiled by DatingNews also report that breakup rates in LDRs are broadly similar to geographically close couples, that the hardest stretch tends to come around month four, and that a meaningful share of couples struggle after reuniting — because they never planned for living together. Treat these as directional, not destiny.

1. Agree on a communication rhythm

More messages isn't automatically better; predictable ones are. Pick a daily anchor (a good-morning text, a 10-minute call at the same time) and a weekly long call. Use the time-zone overlap finder to choose a slot that doesn't wreck anyone's sleep.

2. Always have the next date

Uncertainty is the biggest stressor. A visit on the calendar — even months away — changes how waiting feels. Put it in a shared countdown.

3. Do things, not just talk

Shared activities build the "we" that conversation alone can't. Rotate through virtual date ideas: same-movie nights, cooking the same recipe, co-working calls.

4. Make love tangible

Physical objects bridge the gap: letters, a hoodie that smells like them, a touch bracelet or friendship lamp. Time gifts to the four big festivals — Valentine's, 520, Qixi and Mid-Autumn.

5. Talk about money and visas early

Flights, phone plans and gifts add up. Agree on a shared travel budget and who pays for what. If closing the distance involves moving countries, get qualified professional advice early — rules and timelines vary widely.

6. Learn their language (even a little)

If your partner or their family speaks Chinese, a few phrases — starting with 想你 — show effort that lands hard. Find a tutor.

7. Plan the end of the distance

The healthiest LDRs have an end date or at least a direction: who moves, when, and what each person needs to make it work. Write it down together.

8. Know when to get help

Recurring jealousy, constant conflict, or one person carrying all the effort are signs to talk to a professional. Get matched with a relationship coach.

Quick-start kit: ① set a reunion countdown ② pick your call slot with the time-zone clock ③ take the Miss-You style quiz together ④ send tonight's message.

Source for statistics: DatingNews LDR statistics. This guide is general information, not counselling.
